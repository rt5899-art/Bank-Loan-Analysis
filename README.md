# Bank Loan Analysis

---

## Project Overview

This project presents an **interactive Bank Loan Analysis Dashboard** built using **Excel, Python, and Power BI** to analyze loan applications, funding performance, repayment trends, borrower profiles, and loan quality. The dashboard helps identify lending patterns, monitor portfolio health, and support data-driven financial decision-making through dynamic KPIs and visual analytics.

##  Key Findings & Insights

Based on the data extraction and visualization from the dashboards, here are the key findings regarding the bank's loan portfolio health, borrower behaviors, and risk exposure:

### 1. Portfolio Health & Performance (Good vs. Bad Loans)
* **High Success Rate:** **86.18%** of the applications are categorized as **Good Loans** (Fully Paid or Current), proving that the bank's foundation is financially sound and highly profitable.
* **Loss Exposure:** **13.82%** of loans are classified as **Bad Loans** (Charged Off / Defaulted). While the bank has brought in over `$473M` in total payments against `$435M` funded, default leakage remains a critical area needing strict risk mitigation.
* **Repayment Pipeline:** Month-over-Month (MoM) loan repayment collections grew by **15.8%**, indicating strong cash inflow and an effective collections framework.

### 2. Operational & Behavioral Trends
* **The "Year-End" Seasonal Surge:** Loan applications, funding, and collections show a steady upward trend throughout the year, peaking sharply in **December** (reaching over 4.3K applications). This indicates strong holiday/end-of-year financial demand.
* **Single-Product Vulnerability:** **Debt Consolidation** represents the overwhelming majority of all loan applications and disbursements. While highly profitable, it exposes the bank to intense concentration risk within a single product category.
* **Geographical Over-reliance:** Loan distribution is heavily concentrated in specific states, with **California (CA)** leading significantly in application volume and funding. 

### 3. Borrower Risk Profiles
* **Employment Stability Matters:** Borrowers with **10+ years of employment** receive the highest amount of funding and demonstrate the most reliable repayment patterns. Conversely, borrowers with **<1 year of employment** present a disproportionately high risk of default.
* **Collateral & Stability:** Borrowers with a **Mortgage** or who own their homes represent the safest lending segment. **Renters** show higher debt-to-income (DTI) fluctuations and elevated default frequencies.

##  Recommendations

To maximize profitability, enhance risk controls, and diversify portfolio risk, the following action items are recommended:

###  1. Strengthen Risk Controls for High-Defaulters
* **Tighten Underwriting for Renters and Short-term Employees:** Implement stricter approval criteria or require additional income verification for applicants who rent and have less than 1–2 years of employment stability.
* **Risk-Based Pricing (RBP):** Adjust interest rates higher for applicants showing borderline Debt-to-Income (DTI) metrics or lower credit grades to hedge against potential default losses.

###  2. Product & Regional Diversification
* **Promote Secondary Loan Products:** Reduce product concentration risk in *Debt Consolidation* by actively marketing alternative loan types such as Home Improvement, Credit Card Refinancing, or Small Business loans.
* **Expand Regional Footprint:** Allocate marketing budgets to growing, lower-risk states outside of California (CA) to reduce geographic dependency and build a resilient national portfolio.

###  3. Capitalize on High-Value Segments
* **Target Premium Borrowers:** Launch targeted loan campaigns specifically for long-term employees (10+ years) and mortgage holders by offering competitive interest rates, as they consistently yield low default rates and secure stable returns.
* **Pre-Holiday Campaigns:** Align operational readiness and promotional financing structures around Q4 (specifically October–November) to fully capture and seamlessly process the annual December spike in loan demand.
